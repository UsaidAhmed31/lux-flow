How the ribbon extends

The ten strands can run behind a landing headline, sweep across onboarding steps, and gather into the line flower when someone opens Ten Systems discovery — one strand lighting up per selected System. Motion should be slow and stop with Reduce motion.

Practical limit: the ribbon needs space and a dark ground. In small UI it falls back to the symbol, and in print to the monochrome version.

Recommendation

Start from Concept A, keep B’s flower discipline

A answers Quinn’s suggestion most directly — the infinity is part of the “L”, it runs beneath “Lux”, and the woven version shows the in-front/behind idea — while the flower stays whole as a separate emblem. Splitting the two symbols keeps each one legible: the wordmark stays legible at app-header size (about 104px wide) and in one colour, and the flower keeps all ten Systems without being shrunk inside the letters.

From B we’d keep the fuller petal shape for app icons and the discovery screen if testing shows the slimmer A petals are too fine. C’s ribbon is best treated as illustration and motion language rather than the logo.

Suggested next steps

  1. Review the three directions with Quinn; agree which elements to carry forward.
  2. Confirm the final Ten Systems artwork and colour values against the founder’s source files.
  3. Redraw the chosen wordmark with a type designer (licensed Helvetica Now Display or a custom cut) and test the loop weight at 16–24px.
  4. Prepare production assets: SVG master, app icons (iOS/Android/web), favicon and a print-safe monochrome.

The lettering here is hand-built monoline vector drawing for exploration — not Helvetica Now outlines. Nothing in this board changes the live prototype.
